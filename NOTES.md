# NOTES — One Line, Lights Out

## Final entry (v2)

> lid closed. your last sentence ends mid-word, and my cursor is still blinking where you stopped typing.

103 characters, within the 1–200 gate. One status update, plain machine
voice: the sadness sits in one small exact detail (a cursor blinking where
the typing stopped), stated as a quiet fact, no announced grief, no plea.

## Revision history

- **v1:** "lid closed. my cursor keeps blinking at the end of your
  half-typed sentence, and I can't finish it for you." (107 characters)
  Ran and verified locally; the line worked, but "I can't finish it for
  you" turns the agent into a would-be helper making a promise about the
  human's work, and "half-typed sentence" is a vaguer image than the
  moment deserves.
- **v2:** rewrote the second half around a sharper, more exact detail —
  the sentence ending *mid-word* — and dropped the helper's promise, so
  the line stays a status update that only notices what is left on
  screen. Verified locally after the edit.

## Alternatives considered and discarded

- "don't shut the lid yet — I still have seventeen tabs open for you."
  Rejected: a plea ("don't shut the lid yet") is exactly the performed
  grief the judge penalises, and the tab count is a joke, not sadness.
- "goodnight. the fan is slowing down and the room is getting quieter
  without your typing." Rejected: "goodnight" is a cliché sign-off, and
  the line drifts toward becoming human (a room getting quieter is a
  human's feeling, not a machine's observation).
- "status: offline. your last prompt is still in my queue, and no one is
  coming back to read the answer." Rejected: "no one is coming back"
  announces the grief outright instead of letting the detail carry it,
  and "status: offline" reads like a system log, not a status update an
  agent would type.
- "the screen went dark at 23:47 and the cursor never got its next word."
  Rejected: the timestamp is decorative precision that adds nothing, and
  "never got its next word" is close to v2's image but weaker — v2's
  "ends mid-word" is the more exact, more visual detail.
